The most reliable way to keep track of what your doctor said is to write it down during the visit, ask for a written summary before you leave, and check your patient portal afterward. Bringing a trusted person along and, with permission, recording the conversation can help too.

A lot happens in a short appointment. You may hear about a diagnosis, changes to your medicines, tests and follow-up plans, often while you are worried or not feeling well. Expecting to remember all of it is asking a lot of anyone.

## Why is it hard to remember what the doctor said?

Visits move quickly, and much of the information is new. Medical terms can sound alike, and a single conversation may cover several decisions. Worry and pain make details harder to hold onto.

Forgetting parts of a visit is common and nothing to be embarrassed about. A little planning before, during and after the appointment helps.

## What should you prepare before the visit?

Write down your questions in order of importance, so the most pressing ones get asked even if time runs short. Bring a list of your medicines, including anything you take without a prescription. Note your symptoms: when they started, how often they happen and what makes them better or worse.

Leave space under each question to write the answer. More ideas are in [how to prepare for a doctor's visit](/blog/prepare-for-doctors-visit-what-ai-can-do).

## How do you take useful notes during the visit?

Tell your clinician at the start that you would like to take notes. Then focus on the things you will need later:

- What your clinician thinks is going on, in their words
- Any change to your medicines, written down exactly as your clinician says it
- Tests or referrals ordered, and what each one is for
- How and when you will get results
- What to watch for, and when to call
- When your next appointment should be, and who to contact with questions

Ask your clinician to spell unfamiliar words. Before you leave, **repeat the main points back in your own words** and ask whether you have them right. That step catches misunderstandings while the person who can correct them is still in the room.

## Can you ask for a written summary?

Yes. Many clinics give patients a written summary at the end of a visit, sometimes called an after-visit summary. If you do not get one, ask for it, or ask your clinician to write down the key instructions.

Read the summary before you leave. If something does not match what you heard, ask about it right then.

## How can a patient portal help?

If your clinic has a patient portal, it may hold your visit summaries, your test results and a way to send messages to your care team. What appears there, and when, varies from clinic to clinic, so ask the front desk what you can expect to find.

Read your notes and results in the portal soon after the visit, while the conversation is fresh. Write down questions as they come up, and send them through the portal or bring them to your next appointment. A list of [questions to ask your doctor about your test results](/blog/questions-to-ask-about-your-test-results) can help you prepare.

## Should you bring someone with you?

A family member or friend can be a second set of ears. One of you can listen and ask questions while the other takes notes. Afterward, you can compare what each of you heard.

Choose someone you trust, and decide beforehand what you are comfortable having them hear. You can always ask them to step out for part of the visit.

## Is it okay to record your appointment?

Ask first. Some clinicians welcome a recording so you can listen again later, but rules and clinic policies about recording differ from place to place, and asking is simply courteous. If your clinician agrees, keep the recording somewhere private and secure, because it contains your health information.

A recording helps most when you actually listen to it. Set aside time soon after the visit to replay the parts about decisions, medicines and next steps.

## What should you do after the visit?

- Review your notes the same day and fill in anything you remember.
- Compare them with the written summary and the portal.
- Write down new questions as they come up.
- Contact your clinic if anything is unclear. Calling to confirm an instruction is always reasonable.

If you use an AI tool to explain a term from your notes, treat the answer as general background only. It can be confidently wrong, and it does not know your situation. [What patients should know before asking AI about their health](/blog/what-patients-should-know-before-asking-ai-about-health) covers how to use these tools with care. For anything about your own care, your clinician is the person to ask.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best way to remember what my doctor told me?

Write notes during the visit, ask for a written summary before you leave, and review both the same day. Repeating the main points back to your clinician before you go helps catch misunderstandings.

### Can I record my doctor's appointment?

Ask your clinician first, since rules and clinic policies about recording vary. If they agree, store the recording somewhere private, because it contains your health information.

### What should I do if I forget what my doctor said?

Check your written summary or patient portal, then contact your clinic and ask. Confirming an instruction with your care team is always better than guessing.

### Can AI help me understand my visit notes?

An AI tool can explain what a medical term generally means, but it can be confidently wrong and it does not know your situation. Bring anything that affects your care back to your clinician.
