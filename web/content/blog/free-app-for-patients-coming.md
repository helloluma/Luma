We are building a free iPhone app that helps you hold on to what happens at your doctor's visits: it records the appointment, writes a plain-language summary when you leave, and keeps your results with the visit they belong to. It is in development and not available yet, so this post explains what the app will do, what it will not do, and how to keep track of your visits in the meantime.

## Why are we building an app for patients?

A medical appointment can cover a lot in a short time. In one conversation you might hear the name of a condition, a change to a medicine, the plan for a test and what happens next, often while you are worried, tired or not feeling well. Afterward, it is easy to remember the gist and lose the details. You may realize on the way home that you forgot to ask something, or that you cannot recall exactly what was said about a test.

The rest of Luma is about tracing statements back to their source. Luma checks AI-generated medical answers against published research and flags what it cannot verify. For a patient, the source that matters most is often what your own clinician said in the room, and the app is meant to keep that source close at hand.

## What will the app do?

The app is built around three steps:

1. Record the visit. Tap record when the appointment starts. The app transcribes the conversation and writes a plain-language summary when you leave, so you can read back what was said in words that make sense to you.
2. Keep your results. Take a screenshot of a lab report, a scan or a message from your patient portal, and the app files it with the visit it belongs to. The result and the conversation about it stay together.
3. Look back any time. Every visit stays in one place, so you can see what was said, what changed and what to ask next time.

You sign in with Apple, so there is no password to remember. The app will be free on iPhone.

## What will the app not do?

Like everything Luma makes, the app is not a medical device and does not give medical advice. It will not diagnose anything, and it cannot tell you what a result means for you.

The summary is a record of what was said, written to help you remember it. Automatic transcripts and summaries can mishear a word or leave something out, so treat the summary as a reminder and check anything important with your care team. If a summary seems to say something surprising, ask your clinician before you act on it.

Your clinician remains the person to ask about your health, your results and your treatment.

## Should you ask before recording a visit?

Yes. **Ask your clinician before you start recording, every time.** Rules about recording conversations vary from place to place, and asking first keeps the conversation open and respectful. If the answer is no, take notes instead.

A simple way to ask: "Would it be all right if I record this, so I can remember what we talked about?"

## How can a visit summary help you?

A written summary is most useful when you do something with it soon after the visit:

- Read it the same day, while the conversation is fresh.
- Mark anything you do not understand and write it down as a question.
- Compare it with any written instructions you were given, and if the two differ, ask your care team which is right.
- Go over it with a family member or friend who helps with your care, if you want them involved.
- Bring your questions to your next appointment, or send them through your patient portal if your clinic has one.

## What can you do until the app is ready?

You do not need an app to keep better track of your visits. A few habits help today:

- Write your questions down before the appointment, most important first.
- Bring a current list of your medicines, including vitamins and supplements.
- Take notes during the visit, or bring someone who can take them for you.
- Ask whether a written summary of the visit is available.
- Before you leave, say the plan back in your own words and ask whether you have it right.
